How to Spot a Phishing Attack

Phishing is when an attacker pretends to be someone you trust, like your bank, your school, or a coworker, to trick you into giving up information or clicking a bad link. Most phishing shows up as email or text messages. Here are the warning signs to watch for.

Common Warning Signs

  • A sense of urgency or threats, like "your account will be closed in 24 hours."
  • A sender address that looks almost right but is slightly off, like support@amaz0n.com.
  • Links that do match where they claim to go. Hover over a link before clicking to see the real destination
  • Requests for passwords, payment info, or personal details that a real company would never ask for by email.
  • Spelling and grammar mistakes, or a greeting like "Dear Customer" instead of your name.
  • Unexpected attachments you were not expecting to receive.
